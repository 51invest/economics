Nominal and Real Values

Nominal values use current prices, while real values are adjusted with a price index; only real values support comparisons of quantities across periods.

ModuleECO-M07 · 宏观核算
LevelFoundation
Read5 min · 12 sections

This article has an interactive demo ↓

Definition

Nominal values are measured at current prices. Real values are adjusted for inflation with a price index and reflect changes in quantities or purchasing power. The relationship is

real value = nominal value ÷ (price index ÷ 100) [1].

The GDP deflator is nominal GDP divided by real GDP, multiplied by 100 [1]. A price index equals 100 in its base year and shows the price level relative to that base. Nominal GDP from different years is not directly comparable; only after removing price change can real output be compared [3]. Purchasing-power comparisons require a price index that matches the consumption basket.

Intuition

A monthly salary rises from 5,000 yuan to 5,250 yuan, a nominal increase of 5%. Over the same period the CPI rises from 100 to 107, so prices are up 7%. The real wage is 5,250 ÷ 1.07 ≈ 4,906.54 yuan, down about 1.87%. The nominal number is larger, but it buys less. A price index measures the price level of a basket, not the price of a single item. To judge whether income, output, or asset value really grew, separate price change from quantity change.

Formal expression

real value_t = nominal value_t ÷ (P_t ÷ 100), where P_t is the price index.

GDP deflator = (nominal GDP ÷ real GDP) × 100.

Inflation is π_t = (P_t ÷ P_{t-1} - 1) × 100%; real growth is g_t = (real value_t ÷ real value_{t-1} - 1) × 100%. The base-year index is 100. Rebasing changes the level of a real series but usually not its growth rate. The real interest rate is approximately the nominal rate minus inflation.

When it applies

  1. The index matches the value. The CPI covers a household consumption basket, while the GDP deflator covers all domestic final output; they are not interchangeable [2].
  2. Base year and weights matter. Base years are updated, and basket weights or chain weighting affect the result.
  3. Quality and new goods matter. Quality improvements, new products, and substitution can make an index overstate the rise in the cost of living.
  4. Relative prices change. Deflating everything with one aggregate index hides differences across sectors.
  5. Real values are estimates. Different indexes produce different real values; there is no single correct one.

Scope note: the approximation “real rate ≈ nominal rate − inflation” and the point that the CPI can overstate inflation are not within the supports of the registered sources; where the article says “GDP deflator”, the registered source wording is “GDP price index”, and this article treats them as different names for the same measure rather than as the source’s own term. These are textbook convention and naming convention: a methodological statement, not a conclusion quoted from the registered sources.

Worked example

Suppose nominal GDP is 1,200 in year 1 and 1,500 in year 2, both in 100 million yuan, with GDP deflators of 100 and 125.

Real GDP is 1,200 in year 1 and 1,500 ÷ 1.25 = 1,200 in year 2, in 100 million yuan. Nominal growth is 1,500 ÷ 1,200 - 1 = 25%, while real growth is 0%. Using the CPI instead of the deflator could give a different result. All figures are teaching assumptions.

Common mistakes

Nominal growth equals real growth. They are equal only when the price index is unchanged.

Real values are always below nominal values. That holds only when the price index exceeds 100.

The CPI and the GDP deflator are the same. Their coverage and weights differ.

Deflating wages by the CPI fully restores purchasing power. Taxes, housing, and a household's own basket also matter.

Exercises

  1. Concept check. Which is measured at current prices? A. Nominal GDP; B. Real GDP; C. Constant-price GDP; D. The real wage.
  2. Calculation. A nominal wage rises from 6,000 yuan to 6,300 yuan while the CPI rises from 100 to 108. What are the real wage and its percentage change?
  3. Conditions. Why might the CPI overstate the rise in the cost of living?
Suggested answers
  1. A. The other three are adjusted with a price index.
  2. The real wage is 6,300 ÷ 1.08 ≈ 5,833.33 yuan; the change is 5,833.33 ÷ 6,000 - 1 ≈ -2.78%.
  3. Consumers substitute toward goods that become cheaper, new products enter the basket with a lag, and quality improvements are hard to price; these can make a fixed-basket index overstate the rise in the cost of living.
