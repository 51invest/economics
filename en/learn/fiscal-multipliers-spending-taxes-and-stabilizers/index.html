Fiscal Multipliers: Spending, Taxes and Automatic Stabilizers

The fiscal multiplier is not a fixed constant. It is the feedback chain from a first-round shock to income, consumption and further income, reduced by leakages. Government purchases, tax cuts and transfers have different multipliers; automatic stabilizers cushion downturns, while openness, debt constraints and supply bottlenecks change the result.

By the end of this article, you can compute a spending multiplier from the marginal propensity to consume, the tax rate and the import propensity; distinguish the first-round effects of purchases, tax cuts and transfers; and explain why “multiplier 1.6” is not true at every moment.

Definition

A fiscal multiplier is the change in equilibrium output for a one-unit change in government purchases, taxes or transfers. Government purchases enter aggregate demand directly; tax cuts and transfers raise disposable income first and then become consumption according to the marginal propensity to consume [1]. Automatic stabilizers are tax and transfer changes that respond to the cycle without new legislation, such as progressive income tax, unemployment insurance and low-income support [2].

These are not the same multiplier. The first round of purchases is demand; the first round of a tax cut is income, and only the consumed part becomes demand; transfers resemble tax cuts but may reach households with higher or lower spending propensities. The effect of fiscal stimulus on output and inflation also depends on policy design and the state of the economy, so a single multiplier is not a fixed forecast [3].

Intuition

Suppose the government spends an extra CNY 100 billion on roads. Contractors receive the money and pay wages, materials and profits; workers and suppliers spend part of it, which becomes someone else's income; that income is spent again. Each round leaks through saving, taxes and imports.

If the marginal propensity to consume is 0.6, the tax rate is 0.2 and the import propensity is 0.1, each round's consumption is 0.6 × (1 − 0.2) = 0.48, imports leak 0.1, and 0.38 enters the next round. The multiplier is:

k = 1 / (1 − 0.48 + 0.1) = 1 / 0.62 ≈ 1.61

CNY 100 billion of purchases eventually produces about CNY 161 billion of output. A CNY 50 billion tax cut raises disposable income by CNY 50 billion in the first round and consumption by 0.6 × 50 = CNY 30 billion; the same feedback chain then yields about 30 / 0.62 ≈ CNY 48.4 billion. The tax-cut multiplier is about 0.97, clearly below the purchases multiplier.

Automatic stabilizers work more quietly. If a downturn cuts pre-tax income by CNY 100 billion, a 20% tax rate automatically reduces tax by CNY 20 billion, transfers rise by CNY 5 billion, disposable income falls only CNY 75 billion and consumption falls CNY 45 billion; without stabilizers, consumption would fall CNY 60 billion. No new policy is enacted, but the downturn is cushioned.

Formal expression

In a simplified closed-economy model with a proportional tax and linear consumption, the government-purchases multiplier is:

k_G = 1 / (1 − c(1 − t) + m)

where c is the marginal propensity to consume, t the tax rate and m the marginal import propensity. A change in purchases ΔG changes output by ΔY = k_G × ΔG.

The tax-cut multiplier is:

k_T = −c / (1 − c(1 − t) + m)

so a tax cut ΔT (a negative number) changes output by ΔY = k_T × ΔT. A CNY 50 billion cut is ΔT = −50, so ΔY = −0.6 / 0.62 × (−50) ≈ CNY 48.4 billion.

The transfer multiplier equals the tax-cut multiplier if transfers are fully treated as disposable income and spent at the same propensity. If transfers reach liquidity-constrained households, the marginal propensity may be higher and the multiplier larger; if they reach high-saving households, it is smaller.

The balanced-budget multiplier in the simplified model is:

k_B = (1 − c) / (1 − c(1 − t) + m)

If purchases and taxes both rise by CNY 100 billion, output rises about 0.4 / 0.62 × 100 ≈ CNY 64.5 billion, not CNY 100 billion.

The strength of automatic stabilizers can be written as follows: when income falls by ΔY, tax falls automatically by t × ΔY and transfers rise by s × ΔY, so disposable income changes by (1 − t + s) × ΔY. Larger stabilizers make the first consumption decline smaller.

When it applies

  1. There is spare capacity. Multipliers are larger when demand is weak and resources idle; near full employment, purchases raise prices and rates, and the multiplier shrinks.
  2. Households spend out of income. Liquidity-constrained, low-saving households have high spending propensities and larger multipliers; high-saving households may save a tax cut or repay debt.
  3. Tax and import leakages are stable. Higher tax rates and import propensities reduce the multiplier; small open economies usually have smaller multipliers.
  4. Monetary policy does not offset. If fiscal expansion raises rates and crowds out investment, the net multiplier falls; near the lower bound, fiscal multipliers are usually larger.
  5. Debt is sustainable. High-debt countries may face higher risk premia, and households and firms may expect future tax rises and cut current spending.
  6. Supply bottlenecks are loose. When construction, equipment or labour is scarce, spending turns more into prices than real output.

Worked example

Assume c = 0.6, t = 0.2 and m = 0.1. The denominator is 1 − 0.6 × 0.8 + 0.1 = 0.62, so the purchases multiplier is k_G = 1.61.

CNY 100 billion of purchases raises output by about 1.61 × 100 = CNY 161 billion. A CNY 50 billion tax cut raises first-round consumption by CNY 30 billion and total output by about 30 / 0.62 = CNY 48.4 billion, a multiplier near 0.97. If CNY 50 billion is paid as transfers and recipients also spend 0.6, the effect is close to the tax cut; if recipients spend 0.8, first-round consumption rises CNY 40 billion and output about 40 / 0.62 = CNY 64.5 billion.

Automatic stabilizers: a downturn cuts pre-tax income by CNY 100 billion. Tax falls CNY 20 billion and transfers rise CNY 5 billion, so disposable income falls CNY 75 billion and consumption falls 0.6 × 75 = CNY 45 billion. Without stabilizers, disposable income falls CNY 100 billion and consumption CNY 60 billion. Stabilizers reduce the first-round consumption decline from CNY 60 billion to CNY 45 billion.

If the import propensity rises from 0.1 to 0.2, the denominator becomes 0.72 and the purchases multiplier falls to 1.39, so CNY 100 billion of spending produces only about CNY 139 billion. Near full employment, the multiplier falls further.

These are teaching assumptions, not real fiscal data.

Data and institutional check

Before comparing "Fiscal multiplier formula" with "Spending, tax cuts and transfers", check four things: whether units and denominators match; whether nominal and real, adjusted and unadjusted series have been reconciled; whether the sample is bound by one legal and accounting regime; and whether an independent source can reproduce the order of magnitude.

Three counterexamples

  1. Counterexample 1. Moving the estimated multiplier from one country directly to another: import dependence, tax regime, exchange rate regime and central bank reaction function are all different. Its value is not to reject the whole article but to show where the conclusion starts to need extra evidence.
  2. Counterexample 2. Looking only at federal spending and ignoring simultaneous retrenchment by state and local governments; the net impulse from the combined budget is likely close to zero. Its value is not to reject the whole article but to show where the conclusion starts to need extra evidence.
  3. Counterexample 3. Using a devastated post-war sample to estimate normal-period multipliers mistook the need for reconstruction for a general rule. Its value is not to reject the whole article but to show where the conclusion starts to need extra evidence.

All three point to the same thing: the model is a conditional statement, not an unconditional forecast. State the conditions and the reader knows when to use it and when to stop.

Common mistakes

“The multiplier is a fixed constant.” It varies with the state of the economy. It is larger when resources are idle and smaller near full employment, with high import leakage or high debt risk. Treating 1.6 as a permanent parameter overstates stimulus.

“A CNY 100bn tax cut equals CNY 100bn of purchases.” A tax cut raises disposable income first, and only the consumed part becomes demand; purchases enter demand in the first round. The multipliers differ, and transfers also depend on recipient spending propensities.

“Automatic stabilizers widen the deficit, so they are bad.” A wider deficit in a downturn is the design function of stabilizers; forcing a balanced budget deepens the recession. Read the structural balance, not the cyclical deficit alone.

“Fiscal expansion always crowds out investment.” Crowding out depends on the rate response and spare capacity. When demand is weak and rates are insensitive, crowding out is small; near full employment it is larger.

“Multiplier arithmetic can forecast output precisely.” The model gives direction and policy ranking, not a precise forecast. Expectations, supply bottlenecks, financial conditions and measurement all create error.

Evidence grades: who can check this claim

For "Fiscal multiplier formula", the source can usually support what it is, who publishes it and how it is measured. It cannot automatically support "the same happens in another market". Pull out the third-layer sentences and the reader can see where the risk sits.

For replication, give one person the raw numbers and assumptions and ask them to reach the same order of magnitude. If they cannot, the article is missing a step, not the reader.
