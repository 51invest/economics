Problem and conclusion

Questions and Conclusions

"Concentration Account for Merger and Acquisition Review: HHI, CR4 and Remedial Measures" may seem like a technical detail, but it actually determines a larger judgment: whether we are comparing the same thing or putting together numbers of different calibers. This article uses "HHI" as the entry point, passes through "CR4" and "remedial measures", and finally reaches a reviewable conclusion. The conclusion is not "bigger is better" or "more stable is better", but rather: this indicator can only be used for comparison when the unit, time and institutional boundaries are consistent.

First write the conclusion in three lines: the first line is the direction, the second line is the condition, and the third line is the counter-evidence. If you cannot write three lines, it means that the concept has not been clearly defined, and the subsequent data will only amplify the confusion.

Definitions and boundaries

Definition and Boundaries

There are at least three common definitions of "HHI": stocks, flows, and ratios. The stock looks at the balance at a certain point in time, the flow looks at the changes over a period of time, and the ratio divides the two. Different denominators lead to different conclusions; treating stock growth rate as flow growth is one of the most common mistakes. [1]

"CR4" brings definition into the system. Who is included and who is excluded depends on legal, regulatory and accounting rules. Before making cross-country comparisons, make sure that the samples are subject to the same rules; otherwise, the differences you see may just be institutional differences. [2]

"Remedial measures" determine the boundaries. The indicator is effective within the normal range, but may fail in the extreme range; deleting extreme samples will overestimate stability, and treating extreme samples as the norm will underestimate risk. [3]

Mechanism chain

Mechanism chain

The first chain is "HHI → CR4". The former changes the observable value of the latter, but there may be valuation, time and statistical revisions in between, and you cannot jump to the conclusion in one step.

The second strand is "CR4 → Remedies". The system turns indicators into incentives: the same number corresponds to different behaviors under different rules.

The third chain is feedback. After behavior changes, the indicators themselves will be redefined or recollected, forming a loop of "indicators-behavior-indicators". Ignoring loops will treat endogenous changes as exogenous shocks.

The fourth strand is the border. If any link exceeds the applicable scope, the entire chain will be downgraded to scenario analysis.

Data and conventions

Data and caliber

First unify the units: is the level value nominal or real, what is the denominator of the ratio, and which year is the base period for the index. Then align the time: annual, quarterly and monthly cannot be directly spliced, and the conversion method between seasonal adjustment and non-seasonal adjustment needs to be explained. [4]

Do a revision check again. The initial value, revised value and final value may give different directions; using the final value to backtest the current decision will introduce future information. Finally, do an independent recalculation: change the source and confirm whether the order of magnitude and direction are still consistent.

The four inspections do not require complex models. You only need to write the "denominator, base period, time point, and source" of each number in the table. If you can't write it, don't put it in the conclusion.

Evidence grades

Level of evidence

There are at least four layers of evidence regarding "HHI." The first level is legal, regulatory rules and statistical definitions, which answer “what is counted and what is excluded.” The second layer is data of the same caliber, answering "What is the quantity and direction?" The third level is academic research or official assessment, which answers "whether the mechanism has been identified." The fourth level is the case narrative, which answers "how it happened under specific conditions."

The four layers cannot replace each other. The fact that the rules are clear does not mean that the quantity is established, the quantity is established does not mean that the cause and effect is established, and the vivid cases do not mean that they can be extrapolated. When writing, place each sentence of judgment on one of the levels so that readers can know how much to believe it.

If two layers of evidence conflict, check the caliber first, then the timing, and finally discuss the explanation. Most conflicts are not about "who is right or wrong" but rather two numbers answering different questions.

Timeline and institutional dates

Timeline and institutional time points

Changes in "CR4" tend not to occur evenly. Breakpoints will form when rules take effect, statistical calibers are adjusted, and market structure changes occur. Directly connecting the data before and after the breakpoint into a smooth curve will disguise institutional changes as trends.

There are at least three things you need to do when processing a timeline: indicate the date the rule took effect, indicate the date the data first became available, and indicate the date the revision occurred. When the three dates are inconsistent, use "what you could see at the time" instead of "what you know afterwards" to review the decision.

This is especially true for “remedies.” After extreme events, regulatory and disclosure rules often change, and using post-event parameters to simulate pre-event events will systematically underestimate or overestimate the impact. The timeline is not background decoration, it is part of the identification strategy.

Orders of magnitude and sensitivity

Order of magnitude and sensitivity

Calculate the order of magnitude first, and then talk about the precise value. Calculate the three scenarios where "HHI" changes by 10%, 25% and 50% to see if the conclusion still holds. If a 10% change reverses the conclusion, it means that the conclusion depends on a very narrow parameter interval.

The second step is to do the denominator sensitivity. If the same numerator is replaced by output, population, assets or transaction volume, the ranking may be completely different. The third step is to measure sample sensitivity: delete the largest and smallest 5% samples to see if the mean, elasticity and correlation are stable.

The purpose of sensitivity analysis is not to create a bunch of scenarios, but to find out "which assumption the conclusion is most afraid of." Once you find it, write the assumption as an explicit condition rather than hiding it in a footnote.

Counterfactual and boundaries

Counterfactuals and Boundaries

The counterfactual is not "what if this had not happened" but "what would have happened if all other conditions were equal and only one operable variable was changed." For “CR4,” the actionable variables may be rules, price, inventory, or disclosure frequency, but you cannot change all three at the same time.

A qualified counterfactual should be written clearly: what has changed, what has remained unchanged, which indicator will be used to measure the results, and how long will it take to observe. If one of the four items is missing, the counterfactual becomes a story.

Boundaries are simpler: separate normal ranges, stress ranges, and extreme ranges of "remedial measures." The conclusion of the normal range can be used for daily judgment; the pressure range can only be used for risk warning; the extreme range can only be used for scenario analysis and cannot be used as a baseline forecast.

Three misreadings

Review and next judgment

A review is not a restatement of the results, but an examination of the chain of evidence at the time. Let’s first see whether the definition of “HHI” has been revised afterwards, then see whether the “CR4” system has changed, and finally whether the boundary of “remedial measures” has been crossed.

Write the judgment into a falsifiable sentence: "If A occurs, B should occur within time C; otherwise, the original judgment is invalid." This way, the next time you encounter a similar situation, you will know whether the model is wrong or the conditions have changed.

Keep at least three items in the review record: the sources used at the time, information not known at the time, and the reasons for changing the judgment afterwards. Writing the three items separately can reduce hindsight bias and allow articles in the same module to verify each other.

Application checklist

One page worksheet

  • Topics: HHI, CR4, remedial measures.
  • Caliber: unit, denominator, base period, seasonal adjustment method.
  • System: Applicable subjects, excluded subjects, effective date.
  • Quantity: baseline value, three sensitivities, most feared hypothesis.
  • Evidence: one piece each of rules, data, research, and cases.
  • Conclusion: one sentence each for direction, conditions, and counter-evidence signals.
  • Review: independent sources, recalculation results, qualified opinions.

Having a full worksheet does not mean that the conclusions are reliable; it simply spreads out hidden assumptions so that the next reader can refute them one by one. The worksheet is also not a one-time form, but a record that can be reviewed item by item by the next reader.

Review and the next judgement

Three misjudgments

First, treat “HHI” as a single indicator. It may be a combination of multiple sub-items. When the changes in the sub-items are in the opposite direction to the change in the total amount, the total amount will conceal the real mechanism.

Second, treat the institutional differences in “CR4” as behavioral differences. If the rules are different, the incentives will be different; if the system is not aligned first, the two worlds will be compared.

Third, extrapolate the boundaries of “remedial measures”. Correlations, elasticities and rates in the normal range will change during crises or extreme prices; before extrapolating, ask whether the sample is covered.

One-page worksheet

Application manifest

  • What is the subject, period and unit of this number?
  • Is the denominator output, population, assets or transactions?
  • Have nominal and actual, seasonal and non-seasonal adjustments been unified?
  • Which subjects are covered by the institutional rules and which ones are excluded?
  • Which assumption changes and the conclusion changes?
  • Can another independent source recalculate the order of magnitude?

Checklists are used to turn concepts into actionable judgments, not to replace thinking. Each item must leave a place for evidence, otherwise it will just be a slogan.

Review record

Record three lines: the last access date of the source, the gap between the independent recomputation and the original result, and the opposing view still unresolved. Each line needs a date and a number; a review without a number is an opinion, not evidence for the next judgement. Set the gap threshold before the review and do not move it afterwards. If the gap exceeds it, return to the four-column worksheet instead of adding "broadly consistent" after the conclusion.
